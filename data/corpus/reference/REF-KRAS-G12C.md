# KRAS G12C in solid tumors

AUTHORED REFERENCE NOTE — a short summary written for this demonstration from general domain knowledge. Figures are approximate and are not a substitute for primary literature or clinical guidance.

KRAS G12C, a glycine-to-cysteine substitution at codon 12, is among the more common KRAS variants in lung adenocarcinoma (often cited at around one in eight cases) and is less common in colorectal cancer. Covalent inhibitors that bind the mutant cysteine, such as sotorasib and adagrasib, have been developed for previously treated disease. Trials for these agents frequently require prior platinum-based chemotherapy and/or prior immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy.
